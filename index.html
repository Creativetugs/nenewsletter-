Chapter President’s Message
Arvind Joshi
President, Southwestern Ohio HFMA
2026–2027
Dear Members and Friends,

It is my honor to represent the HFMA Southwest Ohio Chapter for FY26-27, and I look forward to connecting with each of you in the year ahead.

Our industry is evolving at a pace we have never experienced. All of us embracing it at our own pace. That is why I invite every member of our chapter to bring forward your ideas, your solutions, and your vision for what healthcare finance can become. Whether you are new to the profession or have decades of experience, your perspective adds value. When we share openly and collaborate across experience levels, disciplines, and backgrounds, we elevate not only our chapter but our profession. Together, we become better leaders, better partners, and better advocates for the communities we serve.

I encourage our emerging leaders to step forward, get involved, and take full advantage of the opportunities HFMA provides—whether through education, networking, mentorship, or chapter leadership. Your voice matters, your ideas matter, and your participation strengthens our entire chapter. When early careerists engage, we all benefit we gain new viewpoints, fresh momentum, and a stronger, more vibrant professional community.

As we look ahead, let us create an environment where early careerists feel empowered, seasoned professionals feel appreciated, and every member feels connected to a shared purpose. Our strength lies in our collective commitment to learning, growing, and leading with integrity.

I want to extend my sincere appreciation to Sommer Bockerstette for her exceptional leadership and my gratitude to our chapter leaders and volunteers for an outstanding 2025-2026. I especially want to extend my heartfelt thanks to our volunteers—because truly, nothing we do is possible without you. Your time, your passion, and your willingness to step forward are the backbone of our chapter.

I also want to express deep appreciation to our sponsors, whose partnership makes it possible for us to deliver the exceptional education, resources, and networking opportunities our members rely on. We are grateful for your continued collaboration and support.

I look forward to serving alongside you, learning from you, and building a chapter that reflects the very best of who we are and the promise of what we can become. Together, we will continue to elevate our profession, support one another, and make a meaningful impact on the healthcare systems and communities we serve.

To a great year!
MAP Award Winners 2026
The HFMA MAP Keys are the industry standard for measuring revenue cycle excellence. Christ, Mercy Bon Secours, and St. Elizabeth all were MAP winners in 2026 and part of the SWOH chapter.

The 2026 HFMA MAP Award winners include a diverse group of organizations recognized for their high performance in revenue cycle management. Here are some of the notable winners:

Hospital Systems: Roper St. Francis Healthcare, Charleston, S.C.; Bon Secours Mercy Health, Blue Ash, Ohio; St. Elizabeth Healthcare, Edgewood, Ky.; Adena Health System, Chillicothe, Ohio; Valley Health System, Winchester, Va.; Ballad Health, Johnson City, Tenn.; Cottage Health, Santa Barbara, Calif.; Covenant Health, Andover, Mass.

Individual Hospitals: The Christ Hospital Health Network, Cincinnati, Ohio; Corewell Health Reed City, Reed City, Mich.; AnMed, Anderson, S.C.; The University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center, Houston, Texas.

These organizations have demonstrated measurable performance, operational improvement, and long-term commitment to financial practices that support patient care. The HFMA MAP Award is a testament to their dedication to enhancing revenue cycle performance and improving patient satisfaction.

The awards were presented March 18 at the HFMA Revenue Cycle Conference in Arlington, Texas.

Why Volunteer
Benefits of volunteering
Daeylin Rock
Account Director, Tegria
HFMA Programming committee Co-Chair
Daeylin.rock@tegria.com
Tegria | Global Healthcare Consulting & Services Company
HFMA has been such an unexpected surprise in my life. I attended my first event a little over a year ago to network and learn about the current state of healthcare finance in my area. I had no idea that what I thought would be a routine conference would have such a profound impact on my life. Given it was my first event, I didn’t know what to expect. But any anxiety I had was quickly put to rest when I was greeted by such engaged and welcoming chapter leadership. I was blown away, not only by the amazing speakers and content, but how well organized and thoughtful the event was.

I was quickly invited to volunteer on a committee, which I eagerly accepted given the opportunity it presented to learn. From that work, I’ve come to see that every person in this chapter works diligently to ensure that each decision we make is in the interest of our membership and the amazing hospital systems we support. It truly is a community of like-minded individuals who volunteer their time to something they’re passionate about. The vision is clear, and with every event I’ve had the chance to help plan, coordinate, and volunteer at, my appreciation for the work has grown. Like me, many folks join and attend to learn, network, and get a day away from the office. But why do they stay? That’s clear. They stay for the relationships. It’s why it’s such a privilege to be involved. The relationships I’ve built will last long beyond my time as a committee chair, account director, or volunteer. For that, I’m grateful every day.

Certification Spotlight
Why Tera got certified.
Tera Leedy
Manager, Patient Access
Premier Health
My name is Tera Leedy. I am a Manager for Patient Access in Revenue Cycle. Patient Access, which some may refer to as registration, is a part of the front end of the Revenue Cycle. I have held various roles in Patient Access since 2001 from a Patient Access Specialist, Team Lead, Supervisor, and a Manager since 2013.

HFMA’s Certified Revenue Cycle Representative certification is an education for competency that my health system uses for Patient Access Managers. Over my years of experience, I have watched healthcare and the Revenue Cycle change. Regulations, patient responsibility, education to provide to the patient, pricing transparency, denials, and how to create a better experience for patients are just some of the changes I have seen. I first received my CRCR certification in 2013 and have maintained that certification to date. I appreciate the knowledge and big picture of other areas of the Revenue Cycle this certification provides me. As the need changes to communicate to patients upfront, prior to services about pricing transparency, individual benefits, and out of pockets responsibility in a customer centered manner, I have found I am more knowledgeable about these changes, what impact Patient Access has on coding, billing, pre-certifications, and denials. This knowledge assists me in conversation with my direct reports the impact Patient Access has on the patient’s experience, education on regulation and the part Patient Access plays in maintaining compliance, explanation of out of pockets and collections, and the impact our department has on the other departments within the Revenue Cycle.

My plans are to continue enhancing my knowledge by the recertifications of the Certified Revenue Cycle Representative program.

Jeff Burke
Enterprise Account Executive
(OH, MI)
trubridge.com

About TruBridge
TruBridge proudly supports rural and community hospitals and providers in their efforts to stay strong, independent, and deeply rooted in the communities they serve. Backed by more than 45 years of healthcare experience and trusted by over 1,500 clients nationwide, TruBridge offers a mix of technology, services, and strategic expertise – including revenue cycle management, electronic health records (EHR) and analytics – all designed singularly for the realities of rural and community healthcare. With a steadfast commitment to keeping care local, TruBridge helps hospitals flourish as the economic heart of their communities, delivering high-quality, personal care close to home. For more information, visit www.trubridge.com.

Why do you enjoy working in healthcare?
I became “healthcare aware” in 2004. Having spent years in the Auto & Aerospace engineering markets out of UC Mechanical Engineering, applying technology to solving complex process and technical problems, I turned to applying technology and process improvements to healthcare. My first engagement was with TriHealth and applying knowledge-based technology to the registration process. I had the pleasure of working with Yvonne Focke, whom most HFMA members may know. This start has led me on a journey that has never stopped showing me more and more ways to improve healthcare operations for many organizations.

Since that first engagement, I have been impressed by the tenacity and hard work that everyone in healthcare delivers. It just makes you want to help in any way you can to improve technology and process improvements. There has to be a way to make these processes and people more productive and relieve the constant pressure to deliver they all live with. We are all “patients”, so there is a vested interest in making healthcare work efficiently, productively and cost-effectively.

What value do you get from supporting our chapter or being involved in HFMA?
Working with healthcare leaders in our chapter is very rewarding. When you can help them improve and share in a “win-win”, it is rewarding. Uniquely, the SWOH Chapter is very active and there are many opportunities to interact, enjoy, and learn. We all work together to advance the improvements required from an ever-changing market place and government involvement. There is always something to FIX.
